## Verdict

**PASS WITH RECOMMENDED CHANGES.**

The canonical entry correctly distinguishes measurable duration from phonological quantity and correctly warns that orthographic doubling does not prove gemination.

Two additional safeguards are recommended before promotion to `LINGUISTIC_REVIEW`:

1. explicitly distinguish **phonetic duration** from **phonological length/quantity** using specialized literature, not only an introductory textbook;
2. explicitly show that phonetic lengthening can arise without a lexical quantity contrast.

## Evidence for safeguard 1

Odden (2011) characterizes length as the phonological correlate of durational differences and ties it to the phonological concept of quantity. This supports preserving a terminological boundary between physical duration and phonological length.

**Source:** FND010-CAND-001 in `SOURCE-MAP-DURATION-QUANTITY.md`.

## Evidence for safeguard 2

Paschen, Fuchs & Seifart (2022) show that final lengthening is widespread across a 25-language sample and interacts with phonological vowel length. The same segment can therefore be temporally longer because of prosodic position without that fact alone establishing a quantity category.

Oh & Redford (2012) provide a complementary consonantal example: English can contain phonetically long “fake geminates” that are not a general lexical singleton/geminate contrast.

These two sources jointly support the editorial rule:

> longer in time ≠ automatically phonologically long.

## IPA representation

The official IPA chart includes `ː` (long), `ˑ` (half-long) and an extra-short mark among suprasegmentals.

Recommended wording:

> IPA length marks represent temporal/length distinctions in a transcription; the symbol itself does not establish whether the difference is phonemic, allophonic, prosodic or purely phonetic.

## Gemination

The canonical entry's treatment is acceptable, but later language profiles should not define gemination from spelling. A geminate/singleton analysis must come from the language-specific phonological and phonetic evidence.
